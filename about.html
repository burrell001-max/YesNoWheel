About YesNoWheel

Last updated: November 26, 2026

YesNoWheel is a free decision wheel for the small choices that use up an evening.

Why the site exists

Most of what people stall on is not important. It is small, reversible and cheap: where to eat, which film, who goes first, which job nobody wants. The stalling comes from having several decent options and no reason to prefer one. A wheel settles that kind of decision in about two seconds, and it does it in the open, where everyone can see the result was not chosen by whoever suggested the options.

The site exists to do that one job properly on a phone: no account, no install, no paywall. It is built and run by Kent B. Williams, an independent web developer.

What each tool is for

  • The Yes or No wheel answers a question that has two or three possible answers.
  • The name picker draws people for turns, teams, seats and prize draws.
  • The custom wheel takes any list you type, with weights if the draw should not be even.
  • The topic wheels come with their list already loaded: numbers 1 to 20, letters A to Z, the 50 US states, 196 countries, NFL, NBA and MLB teams, food, colour names, the next 14 days and your own pictures.
  • Truth or dare carries 40 truth questions and 40 dares for a game night.

How a result is picked

Every spin asks the browser for a random number through crypto.getRandomValues, and falls back to Math.random only when that is not available. The wheel then animates to the slice that number points at. The decision is made before the animation starts; the spin only shows it. Weights change a slice's chance, elimination mode removes a slice once it wins, and accumulation mode counts wins instead of removing them. There is no hidden rule selecting a result.

Nothing about your wheels is sent anywhere. Entries, titles, colours and pictures are stored in your browser's local storage on your device, and they disappear when you clear site data. There is no account, and no list you build reaches a server. A wheel edited on one device will not appear on another, which is the trade-off for keeping it private.

How the site is funded

The site is free and carries advertising through Google AdSense. Ads are why there is no subscription. Google Analytics records which pages are visited, so I can tell which tools are used and which explanations are not. Both are governed by consent. Visitors in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland choose through Google's consent message before analytics or personalised ads run, and the Privacy Policy explains how to change that answer.

How the blog is written and kept current

Each post covers one narrow decision problem and describes a routine that can be carried out with the tools on this site, in the order the steps appear. A post is dated from the day it was published and shows the date it was last revised; a post that has not changed keeps its original date rather than being re-dated. When a wheel's behaviour changes, the posts describing it are updated in the same edit. If you find something that is wrong or out of date, the contact page is the fastest way to have it corrected.

What the site deliberately leaves out

There is no account, no newsletter sign-up and no place to upload a list for someone else to read. Nothing you type is posted to a server, and no result is sent anywhere. The site does not sell a report, a subscription or a promise that a random answer is the right answer. It exists to settle low-stakes choices quickly, in the open, with the reasoning shown rather than hidden. When a decision needs evidence, expertise or another person's consent, the wheel is the wrong instrument and the guides say so.

Who runs this site

YesNoWheel is built and run by Kent B. Williams, an independent web developer. The wheels and the guides are that one person's work, and messages sent through the contact page reach the same person who writes the pages.
