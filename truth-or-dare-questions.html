Truth or Dare Wheel

        Eighty prompts, sorted into four groups

        This page is a question bank first and a wheel second. There are 40 truth questions and 40 dare challenges, ten of each for four audiences: kids, family, friends and a party. Every prompt is written to work with what is already in the room, without anyone leaving it, and without anyone having to touch a phone for longer than a message.

        The wheel arrives already loaded with all 80, each one prefixed Truth or Dare so the slice tells you which you are getting before it stops. That is a 1-in-80 draw per prompt and an even 40-in-80 chance of a truth, which is not the same as a coin toss: the split is fixed by the list, not by a coin.

        Categories are in the list, not on the wheel. If you want a kids-only round, open the text view and delete the lines from the other three groups. The wheel redraws with what is left.

        How to run a round

        Spin with the button, the space bar, or a click on the hub at the centre of the wheel. Read the prompt out loud exactly as it stands; paraphrasing is how a dare becomes something the group was not expecting.

        • One spin per turn. Whoever spun takes what the wheel gives them, or uses their pass. Passes are agreed before the first spin, not after a dare lands badly.
        • Elimination mode offers Hide choice once a prompt has been used, so a long evening does not repeat the same three questions.
        • The text view is the filter: keep the kids list for a young group, keep only party prompts for a grown-up evening, or paste in your own questions.
        • Mystery wheel hides the labels until the spin stops, which stops anyone shopping for a prompt they like.

        Set the boundary before the first spin

        Every group has a version of this game that ends badly, and it is almost always a dare that needed a prop, a photo or someone else's consent. Settle three things out loud first: anyone can pass once without explaining; nothing leaves the room (no posting, no filming); and a dare that needs someone else's permission is not a dare, it is a request.

        The prompts here stay inside those rules. They are loud, silly and occasionally embarrassing in a harmless way, but none of them requires alcohol, a purchase or going outside, and the two that involve another person, the conga line and the invented handshake, are offers anyone can decline. If your group wants a different edge, write your own list rather than stretching these into something they were not written for.

        End the round while people are still laughing. Two rounds of 15 minutes is usually better than one that runs until everybody is tired and the dares get worse.

        The 40 truth questions

        Kids

        • What is the best thing you have ever built or made?
        • Which cartoon character would you want as a friend, and why?
        • What is the silliest thing you are afraid of?
        • What is your favourite thing to do on a rainy day?
        • What is the best present you have ever given someone?
        • Which animal would you be for a day?
        • What is something you are proud of that nobody here knows about?
        • What is the funniest thing a pet has done in your house?
        • What is a food you pretended to like but did not?
        • If you could have one superpower, but only on Tuesdays, which would you pick?

        Family

        • What is the best meal anyone in your family cooks?
        • Which family tradition would you keep forever?
        • What is one small thing someone in this room does that you appreciate?
        • What is the worst haircut you have ever had?
        • What is the last thing that made you laugh out loud?
        • Which household job do you actually not mind?
        • What is the best trip you have taken with family?
        • What is a song you know every word to?
        • What is the longest you have gone without your phone?
        • What is something you believed as a child that turned out to be wrong?

        Friends

        • What is the first thing you noticed about the person on your left?
        • What is a small habit your friends tease you about?
        • What is the best advice a friend has given you?
        • Which friend would you call first in a crisis, and why?
        • What is a message you still think about?
        • What is the most useless skill you have?
        • What is the last thing you apologised for?
        • What is a compliment you remember word for word?
        • What is the worst outfit you have worn in public?
        • What is something you have changed your mind about this year?

        Party

        • What is the best party you have been to, and what made it good?
        • What is your go-to dance move when nobody is watching?
        • What is the strangest food combination you genuinely enjoy?
        • What is the longest you have stayed up, and why?
        • What is a game you always lose?
        • What is the most embarrassing song in your playlist?
        • What is the worst gift you have received, and did you keep it?
        • What is the biggest mess you have made in a kitchen?
        • What is the last thing you fixed yourself?
        • What is one rule you would add to every party?

        The 40 dare challenges

        Kids

        • Do your best impression of an animal until someone guesses it.
        • Balance a book on your head while counting to twenty.
        • Say the alphabet backwards as far as you can.
        • Do ten jumping jacks and finish with a bow.
        • Draw the person to your right in thirty seconds.
        • Speak in a robot voice until your next turn.
        • Name five blue things in the room in ten seconds.
        • Dance for fifteen seconds with no music.
        • Sneeze like three different animals.
        • Hop on one foot while saying the months of the year.

        Family

        • Sing the chorus of a song everyone here knows.
        • Do an impression of a family member until someone guesses who it is.
        • Tell a joke with a straight face.
        • Give a one-minute speech on why spoons beat forks.
        • Do five squats while quoting your favourite film line.
        • Balance a spoon on your nose for ten seconds.
        • Talk in an accent until your next turn.
        • Make up a two-line rhyme about the last thing you ate.
        • Act out your morning routine in mime.
        • Pay everyone in the room one compliment, one sentence each.

        Friends

        • Let the group choose a nickname for you for the next three rounds.
        • Message someone outside the group a single emoji and nothing else.
        • Do an impression of the person to your left.
        • Speak only in questions until your next turn.
        • Read your last sent message aloud in a dramatic voice.
        • Give a twenty-second weather report about this room.
        • Hum a song and let the group guess it in three tries.
        • Hold a plank while naming five capital cities.
        • Describe your day as if it were a film trailer.
        • Do an impression of how you sound when you are tired.

        Party

        • Start a conga line and keep it going for one full round.
        • Walk a catwalk across the room and back.
        • Invent a handshake with the person on your right.
        • Say a tongue twister three times without stopping.
        • Do a ten-second stand-up set about the last queue you stood in.
        • Freeze in a dramatic pose until someone copies you.
        • Do twenty seconds of air guitar with sound effects.
        • Balance on one leg while naming five pizza toppings.
        • Teach the group a dance move in thirty seconds.
        • Narrate the next two minutes of the party like a sports commentator.

        Questions people ask

        Is it exactly half truths and half dares?

        Yes. The wheel holds 40 of each, 80 prompts in total, so a single spin is a 40-in-80 chance of a truth. Add or delete lines and the split follows your list.

        Are these suitable for children?

        The kids and family groups were written to be, and nothing in the bank needs a bought prop, money or leaving the room. Read the list before you play: you know your group better than any page does.

        Can I add my own questions?

        Yes. Open the text view, add one prompt per line, and start each with Truth: or Dare: so the slices stay readable.

        What if someone does not want to answer?

        They pass. Agree the pass rule before the first spin, and do not make anyone explain why they used it.

        Does the wheel repeat prompts?

        In normal mode it can. Switch to Elimination mode and hide each prompt when you want it to appear once, which matters for a long game night.

        Is it free and does it need an account?

        It is free and there is no account. An edited list lasts for the visit, because the 80 prompts are reloaded each time the page opens.
