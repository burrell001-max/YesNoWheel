What Are the Best Air-Purifying Indoor Plants for Your Home?

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Plants do clean tiny amounts of air, but the real payoff is easier than that: they are good company. Here are five tough ones worth buying.

The honest science

The idea comes from a 1989 NASA study that sealed plants in small chambers and watched pollutants drop. In a real home, with open doors and moving air, you would need a jungle to match what a window does. Treat plants as a bonus for your mood and your space, and use ventilation for air quality.

Five that are hard to kill

Snake plant tolerates low light and missed waterings. Pothos trails nicely and forgives almost everything. Spider plant makes baby plants you can pot up and give away. Peace lily tells you when it is thirsty by drooping. Aloe likes a sunny sill and a gritty, fast-draining soil.

Keep pets in mind

Several popular houseplants, including pothos, peace lily and aloe, are toxic if chewed by cats or dogs. Check the ASPCA plant list before you buy, and put anything doubtful out of reach.

A simple plan

Start with one plant, near the window it suits. Water only when the top inch of soil is dry. Add a second plant after a month if the first is still happy.
