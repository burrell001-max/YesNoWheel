Yes or No Wheel – Free Yes No Generator & Decision Spinner

        Free Yes or No Wheel and Random Yes or No Generator With No Sign-Up

        This is a free yes or no wheel and random yes or no generator you can spin in one click, plus a full custom decision maker wheel for names, tasks, prizes or anything else you need to choose between. It is a simple yes or no spinner on the surface, with the options of a full yes no picker wheel underneath, including a yes no maybe wheel mode. Everything runs in your browser, so there is nothing to install and no account or sign-up to create.

        Use the Yes/No Wheel tab for a quick answer, or switch to Custom Wheel to build your own list. The sections below explain how each part works, so you know what you are getting before you spin.

        How the Spin Is Decided

        Each spin picks its result first, then animates the wheel so it lands on that slice. The pick uses your browser's built-in random number generator (crypto.getRandomValues), which is designed for unpredictable output, and falls back to a standard random function only if your browser does not support it. Spin speed, spin length and the physics of the pointer are visual only. They do not change which slice wins.

        On the Yes/No wheel every slice has the same chance. On a custom wheel, an entry with a weight of 2 is twice as likely to win as an entry with a weight of 1, so you can make a wheel where some choices are deliberately more likely than others.

        Yes No Maybe Wheel and Repeated Spins

        The default wheel gives a firm Yes or No. Turn on the third option and it becomes a Yes, No or Maybe wheel, which fits questions where "not yet" is a fair answer, such as "Should I decide tomorrow?" With three equal slices, each answer has a one in three chance on every spin.

        Use Yes/No when you want the wheel to settle a question, and Yes/No/Maybe when you want a nudge that leaves room to think. A Maybe result is a useful signal in itself: it often means the choice matters less than you thought, or that you need one more piece of information first.

        You can also spin up to five wheels at once. This is useful when you want a tally instead of a single result, for example asking the same question five times and going with the majority. A running scoreboard counts every result, and the Results tab keeps a history you can export as CSV.

        Is the Yes or No Wheel Really 50/50?

        Yes. As a random yes or no generator, it is fair. The Yes/No wheel has two slices of equal size, and each spin picks one of them with an equal 50% chance, using your browser's secure random number generator. The result does not depend on where the wheel stopped last time, how hard you click, or how long the spin runs.

        Each spin is independent, so past results do not change the next one. Streaks still happen: five Yes results in a row come up about once in every 32 sequences of five spins. That is normal randomness, not a sign that the wheel is broken or "due" for a No. If you want uneven odds on purpose, build a custom wheel and give an entry a higher weight.

        Yes or No Wheel vs a Coin Flip

        A coin flip and a Yes/No wheel both give a 50/50 answer, so for a single quick decision either one works. The wheel adds a few things a coin cannot.

        • More than two outcomes. Add Maybe, or switch to a custom wheel with any number of choices.
        • Weighted odds. Make one answer more likely than another when a straight 50/50 is not what you want.
        • A visible record. A scoreboard and a results history show what the wheel has picked, which settles "wait, what did it say last time?"
        • A shared screen. A group can watch the same wheel on a projector or phone, and you can share the wheel with a link.

        A coin is faster if you have one in your pocket. A wheel is better when you want a group to see the decision, keep a record, or go beyond Yes and No.

        Build a Custom Picker Wheel

        Open the Custom Wheel tab to enter your own entries. You can type them one at a time or paste a whole list in text view, using one entry per line, or a weight and a name separated by a comma. Each entry can have its own color, image and weight, and you can hide, duplicate, delete or drag entries into a new order.

        • Import and export: load a list from a CSV, TXT or JSON file, and export your list or spin history when you are done.
        • Elimination mode: remove each winner after it is picked, so everyone gets a turn exactly once. This suits seating, presentation order and chore rotations.
        • Accumulation mode: keep a live leaderboard of how often each entry has won.
        • Mystery options: hide the entry names on the wheel, or hide the result until you reveal it, to add suspense for a group.
        • Shuffle, sort and search help keep long lists manageable.

        Tips for a Better Answer

        Ask one clear question

        "Should I cook dinner tonight?" works better than "What should I do tonight?" Both Yes and No need to be real, acceptable answers.

        Set your rule before you spin

        Decide in advance whether the first result is final or whether you will go best of three. Without a rule, it is easy to keep spinning until you hear what you wanted.

        Notice your reaction

        If the wheel says No and you feel disappointed, you have learned what you wanted. You can follow the wheel or follow that feeling.

        Keep it low-stakes

        Do not use a random result for health, safety, money or legal decisions. Use reliable information and a qualified professional for those.
