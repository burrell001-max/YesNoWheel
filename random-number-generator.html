Number Picker Wheel

        Twenty numbers, one to twenty

        The wheel loads with the numbers 1 to 20, one per slice, in order. Each number has the same 1-in-20 chance, and a number that has just won is no less likely to win again unless you switch modes.

        Twenty is a useful default because it covers most small draws: a twenty-sided die, a class of twenty, twenty minutes, twenty points. It is also short enough that the labels stay large on a phone.

        How to use it

        Press the spin button, use the space bar, or click the hub at the centre of the wheel. The chosen number fills a pop-up, sized to be read from across a table.

        • The text view replaces the list. Type 1 to 6 for a die, 1 to 100 for a percentage, or 0 to 9 for digits.
        • Elimination mode offers Hide choice after each draw, so five spins can give five different numbers. That is the mode for seating orders and unique picks.
        • Accumulation mode keeps every number in play and counts the draws, which turns the wheel into a visible tally for a quiz.
        • Weights can double a number's slice when the draw is meant to be biased, for example a handicap in a game.

        Five ways to use a number draw

        Dice replacement

        Put 1 to 6 on the wheel and spin instead of rolling. It is useful when a board game has lost its die, and it is visible to everyone at the table, which matters in a game where people suspect each other.

        Scoring

        Spin for points after a round: 1 to 20 gives a spread wide enough to matter and small enough to stay fair over a short game.

        Chore or seat order

        In elimination mode, spin once per name and read the numbers out as the order. Nobody has to argue about who goes first because the sequence was drawn in front of them.

        Quiz points

        Draw a number for how many points the next question is worth, or for how many seconds the team has to answer. It keeps a quiz from settling into the same rhythm.

        Random pick from a numbered list

        Number a list of options 1 to 20, spin, and match the result to the number. It is the quickest way to draw from something you cannot easily type out.

        What the default range is for

        1 to 20 is a starting point, not a limit. The wheel does not generate a number between two values you choose; it draws from the list on the wheel. So if you need 1 to 50, put 50 lines in the text view, or type only the numbers that are actually in play.

        Typing a short list is usually better than a wide range. A draw from six options tells everyone what the possibilities are, while a draw from 1 to 100 mostly produces a number that means nothing until someone looks it up.

        Questions people ask

        Can I change the range?

        Yes. Edit the list in the text view: 1 to 6 for a die, 1 to 100 for a percentage, or any set of numbers you need.

        Does it avoid repeating a number?

        Not in normal mode; each spin is independent. Elimination mode offers Hide choice once a number wins, so a run of draws cannot repeat.

        Can I draw two numbers at once?

        Not in one spin. Spin twice, or use elimination mode so the second draw cannot match the first.

        Is the draw truly random?

        The wheel picks a slice with your browser's random number generator and then animates to it. It is fine for games and classroom use; it is not a certified lottery draw.

        Can I make some numbers more likely?

        Yes, with weights. A number with weight two gets twice the chance of a number with weight one.

        Is it free?

        Yes, with no account and no limit on spins.
